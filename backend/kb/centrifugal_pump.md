## [PUMP-1.1] Normal operating limits
Vibration: warning at 4.5 mm/s, critical at 7.1 mm/s. Bearing housing temperature: warning at 75 C, critical at 90 C. Discharge pressure: warning at 9 bar, critical at 11 bar. Operation above critical limits requires immediate shutdown inspection.

## [PUMP-2.1] Fault: high vibration
Possible causes: bearing wear, shaft misalignment, impeller imbalance, cavitation, loose foundation bolts. Cavitation is usually accompanied by a crackling or gravel-like noise and unstable suction pressure. Bearing wear typically shows rising vibration with rising bearing temperature.

## [PUMP-2.2] Fault: high bearing temperature
Possible causes: insufficient or degraded lubricant, over-greasing, bearing wear, misalignment, blocked cooling. Check lubricant level and condition before disassembly.

## [PUMP-2.3] Fault: low or fluctuating discharge pressure
Possible causes: cavitation, clogged suction strainer, worn impeller, air ingress at suction, partially closed suction valve, internal recirculation from worn wear rings.

## [PUMP-2.4] Fault: leakage at shaft seal
Possible causes: worn mechanical seal faces, dry running, shaft deflection from misalignment, thermal damage from overheating.

## [PUMP-3.1] Inspection steps: vibration and temperature faults
1. Isolate and lock out the pump before physical inspection. 2. Check coupling alignment with a dial gauge or laser tool. 3. Inspect foundation and baseplate bolts for looseness. 4. Check bearing lubricant level and condition. 5. Listen for cavitation noise and inspect the suction strainer. 6. Inspect impeller for damage or build-up if vibration persists.

## [PUMP-3.2] Maintenance priority guidance
Critical: any reading at or above critical limit, or visible leakage with abnormal noise. High: warning-level readings on two or more parameters. Medium: single warning-level reading. Low: routine observations with all readings normal. Missing or conflicting sensor data must be verified before the priority is lowered.
